## Segment 1 — Foundational Prerequisites & Propulsion Architecture

### 1.1 Required Competencies

Students enrolled in this curriculum are assumed to possess a graduate-level understanding of thermodynamics, electromagnetism, and fluid mechanics. Prior to engaging with Module 1, students must have completed or tested out of the following prerequisites:

- ****Reactor Thermodynamics & TPC Conversion (DSPL-PWR-782):**** Understanding of the Fission Core thermal output limits, the Thermophotovoltaic (TPC) intermediate conversion loop, and the Hybrid Energy Storage System (HESS) architecture.
- ****ISCD Electromagnetic Field Theory (ASTRO-401):**** Baseline understanding of the magnetic field coils used in Internal Spacetime Compression Drelineation (ISCD) actuation, as these same coils are repurposed for exhaust vectoring.
- ****Materials Science under Extreme Radiation:**** Familiarity with the Aft Radiation & Thermal Exclusion Zone (AR-TEZ) constraints and the physical limits of high-neutron-flux environments.

This course does not teach reactor design, nor does it certify students in ISCD warp mechanics. The focus is strictly on the conversion of reactor thermal output and fissile byproducts into directed kinetic thrust for sub-light (STL) maneuvering.

### 1.2 The Propulsion Architecture Paradigm

In atmospheric and early-stage planetary aviation, thrust is generated through chemical combustion—the burning of a fuel with an oxidizer. In the vacuum of interstellar space, the mass ratios required for chemical propulsion become thermodynamically impossible for vessels exceeding 20kt dry mass.

Modern Terran starship propulsion abandons chemical combustion entirely. Instead, vessels utilize the immense thermal and electrical output of their Primary Fission Cores to accelerate reaction mass out the aft of the ship. The efficiency and scale of this acceleration are governed by the ****Lorentz force****—the interaction of electrical currents and magnetic fields.

Because modern Fission Cores utilize a 50% U-235/238 Fluoride Eutectic Salt Matrix as their primary fuel, a continuous supply of highly radioactive, superheated liquid salt is available as a byproduct. This course details how the Magnetic Pinch Drive (MPD) weaponizes this byproduct, converting the reactor's waste into a high-velocity plasma exhaust stream.

### 1.3 Course Overview & Syllabus Trajectory

The following modules will progress from low-power precision maneuvering to the extreme thermodynamics of orbital escape. Students will be evaluated on their mathematical comprehension of mass flow rates, magnetic field geometries, and thermal loading limits.

- ****Module 2: Tier III Propulsion (Cold Electron & Aux Ion Discharge)**** — Focuses on low-thrust, high-precision maneuvering utilizing TPC-stored electrical power to drive electrostatic micro-thrusters.
- ****Module 3: Tier II Propulsion (Hydrogen Plasma Thermal Burn)**** — Covers the routing of reactor thermal output through heat exchangers to superheat compressed hydrogen for standard sub-light transit.
- ****Module 4: Tier I Propulsion (The Magnetic Pinch Drive)**** — The core focus of this curriculum. Explores the foundational thermodynamics proposed by First-Era physicist Robert Zubrin, whose mathematical models for continuous salt-slurry thermal expansion laid the groundwork for modern high-thrust drives. Students will learn how modern magnetic nozzles solve the historical material-tolerance barriers of Zubrin's era by containing the 400+ Gigawatt fission reaction purely via electromagnetism.
- ****Module 5: Fissile Plumbing & AR-TEZ Dynamics**** — The logistics of pumping critical-mass eutectic salts from anti-criticality vaults to the ignition chamber, and the thermal management of the radioactive exhaust plume.

__Note: Students are advised that miscalculation of magnetic pinch field geometries during the Module 4 laboratory simulations will result in immediate plasma containment failure and a simulated catastrophic hull breach. Review your Lorentz force equations carefully.__
